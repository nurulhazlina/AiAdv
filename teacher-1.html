Why AI? From Rules to LearningKenapa AI? Dari Peraturan kepada Pembelajaran

⏱ 75–90 minutes 👥 Pairs or individual 🖥 1 device per student 📋 Bilingual (EN / BM)
💡

Big Idea & Learning Objectives

Start here
The Central Insight / Idea Utama

Students discover why machine learning exists — because some problems are too complex to solve with hand-written rules — and learn to distinguish supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning.

Pelajar mengetahui sebab pembelajaran mesin wujud — kerana sesetengah masalah terlalu kompleks untuk diselesaikan dengan peraturan tulisan tangan — dan belajar membezakan pembelajaran diselia, tidak diselia, dan pengukuhan.

Module
Module 1
Duration
75–90 minutes
Tiers
Cal → 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → MCQ → Reflect
  • Explain why ML is preferred over rule-based systems for complex tasks / Terangkan mengapa PM lebih diutamakan daripada sistem berasaskan peraturan untuk tugasan kompleks
  • Classify examples of supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning / Klasifikasikan contoh pembelajaran diselia, tidak diselia, dan pengukuhan
  • Justify ranking decisions using evidence from data requirements / Justifikasikan keputusan peringkat menggunakan bukti daripada keperluan data
  • Critique an analogy and construct an alternative explanation / Kritik analogi dan bina penjelasan alternatif

🎯

Calibration Phase / Fasa Kalibrasi

~5–8 min
What students do

Rate AI/ML confidence (1–10), predict their Tier 1 score, and write ≥10 words on what they already know about the topic.

Facilitator notes
  • Emphasise "no wrong answers here" — calibration is for self-awareness, not grading
  • Watch for students who claim 10/10 confidence — they may rush Tier 1; encourage deeper justifications
  • Students who write less than 10 words are blocked — walk over and prompt: "Tell me one thing you've heard about AI"
  • Collect calibration data later to compare with post-activity Reflect scores — excellent metacognitive evidence

Tier-by-Tier Guide / Panduan Tahap demi Tahap

Core activity
1
Tier 1: Concepts / Konsep
⏱ 15–20 min
Four concept questions: Concept+Justify, Rank+Reason, True/False+Justify, Analogy Selection. Each answer requires a written justification (min 8 words).
Expected

Q1: Supervised learning (labelled examples)

Expected

Q2: A (voice emotion) > C (recommendation) > B (cart total — fixed formula, not ML)

Expected

Q3: False — ML programs itself from data

Expected

Q4: B — the child learning from examples

Facilitator Tips
  • Circulate while students write their justifications — push for specifics ('because…', 'for example…')
  • For Q2 ranking, accept A>C>B or C>A>B with strong reasoning; the key is B last.
  • If a student picks 'chef' in Q4, ask: 'Does a chef change the recipe based on tasting results?'
2
Tier 2: Fix / Baiki
⏱ 15 min
Students identify and correct errors in a flawed ML scenario description. Tests whether they can spot category mistakes and mislabelled learning types.
Expected

Multiple items may be wrong; accept reasoned corrections

Expected

Watch for confusing 'training accuracy' with 'test accuracy'

Facilitator Tips
  • Encourage students to annotate before rewriting
  • Ask: 'What clue in the scenario tells you which learning type it is?'
3
Tier 3: Extend / Kembang
⏱ 15 min
Students apply concepts to a new scenario — designing which ML type would suit a Malaysian school app that recommends tuition classes.
Expected

Supervised (labelled outcome data) is most defensible

Expected

Unsupervised (finding student clusters) is also acceptable with justification

Facilitator Tips
  • Encourage real Malaysian context (UPSR/PT3/SPM data, school types)
  • Prompt: 'What labelled data would you need? Who would label it?'
4
Tier 4: Create / Cipta
⏱ 15–20 min
Students design a simple ML-powered app for their school or community. Must specify: problem, learning type, data source, and potential risks.
Expected

No single correct answer — assess coherence, specificity, and awareness of risks

Facilitator Tips
  • Common strong answers: attendance predictor, food waste reducer, Bahasa Malaysia grammar checker
  • Push students to name their data source ('millions of…') not just say 'AI collects data'
  • If time allows, have pairs swap designs and critique each other's risk section
⚠️

Common Misconceptions / Salah Faham Biasa

Watch for these
✗ "All AI is ML"
✓ AI is a broad field; ML is one approach. Rule-based chatbots are AI but not ML.
✗ "More data always = better model"
✓ Quality, diversity and relevance of data matter as much as quantity.
✗ "Supervised = supervised by a human always watching"
✓ The 'supervision' is the labelled dataset, not live human oversight.
✗ "RL is just gaming"
✓ Reinforcement learning is used in robotics, logistics, drug discovery, etc.
💬

Debrief & Discussion / Perbincangan & Rumusan

Closing 10 min
Show of hands: who changed their answer during the activity? What made you change?
Name one real app on your phone you now think uses ML. Which type?
What would happen if someone used a rule-based system to detect exam cheating — why would it fail?
🎛️

Differentiation / Pembezaan

Inclusive support
Support (struggling students)

Provide a vocabulary card: AI, ML, supervised, unsupervised, reinforcement, label, training data. For Q2 ranking, let struggling students work in pairs.

Extension (advanced students)

Ask advanced students: 'Could the same task use more than one ML type? Design a hybrid approach.'

📊

Assessment Notes / Nota Pentaksiran

Evidence of learning
Look for in Tier 1
  • Justification goes beyond restating the option chosen
  • Student names a specific reason tied to the concept (labels, data type, task complexity)
  • Ranking explanation references WHY the bottom task doesn't suit ML
Look for in Tier 4 (Create)
  • Problem is specific and scoped (not "solve all of education")
  • At least one risk or limitation is mentioned unprompted
  • Data source is realistic and ethically considered
Reflect section signals
  • Compare calibration confidence (pre) vs reflect score (post)
  • Students who over-estimated often have surface understanding
  • Use reflection responses in next lesson's warm-up
